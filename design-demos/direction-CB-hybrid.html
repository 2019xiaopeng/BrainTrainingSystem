About the test

One click, one honest number.

The reaction time test measures how fast you respond to a change on screen. Click to start, hold through the red field, and click again the instant it flips green. Your time is the gap between the flip and your click — nothing else. You get ten attempts and your median is the score, so one lucky or one fumbled click doesn't decide it.

Screens, mice and touch layers each add a few milliseconds of their own — typically 20–50 ms combined — so treat your number as a way to compare against yourself over time, not a lab measurement. Compare scores from the same device only, and if you consistently land well under 100 ms, you're anticipating the flip rather than reacting to it.

FAQ

Good to know.

Is this really free, with no account?

Yes. Every game runs entirely in your browser — nothing to install, no signup, no email. Your scores are stored on your device and nowhere else.

What counts as a good score?

Public statistics at humanbenchmark.com put the median player at 273 ms across roughly 81 million attempts. Most people land between 250 and 300 ms — and beating your own number from last week is the part worth tracking.

Why is my phone slower than my laptop?

Touch sampling and screen refresh add latency. The same nervous system usually reads 20–50 ms slower on a phone — compare your scores from the same device only.

Does a fast time mean my brain is better?

No. It measures one specific thing — a visual-motor response on whatever device you're holding. It is not an IQ result, not a percentile rank, and not a “cognitive score”. The other eight games exercise entirely different skills.
