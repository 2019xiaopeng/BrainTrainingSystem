01 — About the test

What this test measures

This is a simple visual reaction time test. The screen holds, a signal appears, and you tap as fast as you can. The interval between the signal and your tap — measured in milliseconds — is your score. Most first attempts land between 230 and 300 ms.

Your number includes device latency, not just your brain. The display needs time to draw the signal and the browser needs time to register your tap — typically 20–50 ms combined. A desktop monitor with a mouse or keyboard reads closest to true reaction time; touchscreens usually read slightly slower. Treat your score as a consistent personal baseline, not a lab measurement.

Reference point: the typical value of 273 ms is the median of roughly 81 million samples published publicly by humanbenchmark.com. It is crowd data from their visitors — a useful yardstick, not a clinical norm. Your own runs are kept in your browser's local storage: no account, no upload, nothing leaves this page.

What counts as a good reaction time?+

The public median is 273 ms. Regular players — gamers, athletes — often sit between 200 and 250 ms. Consistency across five taps matters more than one lucky tap.

Why is my phone slower than my laptop?+

Touch sampling and screen refresh add latency. The same nervous system usually reads 20–50 ms slower on a phone — compare your scores from the same device only.

Do you store my results anywhere?+

In your browser's local storage, and nowhere else. Clear your browser data and they are gone — there is no server-side account to sync to.
